# TikTok Carousel — What's Actually in an AI Agent (Part 1 of 2)

**Format:** TikTok photo mode, 9:16 (1080×1920)
**Framework:** Value-Stack, restructured as a cumulative build (one scene assembled slide by slide, not parallel definitions)
**Slides:** 6
**CTA:** Follow — explicitly for part 2
**Series:** Part 1 explains the three primitives (agent, skill, MCP server). Part 2 builds an agentic workflow out of them.
**Metaphor:** A chef in a kitchen — chosen over "workshop" and "RPG character" for broad, non-technical relatability while still mapping 1:1 onto the three concepts.

## Slide 1 — Cover

> **An AI "agent" is
> a chef in a kitchen.**
>
> Here's what's actually
> standing in the room.

**Visual:** A simple line-art chef figure, centered, in an otherwise empty kitchen outline — counter, a bare shelf, a bare wall hook. Both the shelf and the hook are empty/silhouetted, visually promising they'll be filled in.

**Why it works:** Curiosity hook, not a value hook — it promises a scene, not a list. The empty shelf and empty hook are the visual "count" even though no number is stated; the viewer can see two more things are coming before they read a word about it.

**Visual:** Zoom in on the chef figure. Two labels pointing at them: "brain — decides" (pointing at head) and "hands — acts" (pointing at hands). Kitchen fades to background.

**Why it works:** Demystifies the word "agent" immediately — it's a decision-maker with the ability to act, not a black box. This is the slide most likely to be someone's first correct mental model of the term.

## Slide 3 — The skill

> **The recipe.**
>
> The chef doesn't memorize
> every dish in the world.
>
> They pull the recipe off
> the shelf *when that dish
> comes up* — then put it back.
>
> That's a skill: knowledge
> that loads only when the
> job calls for it.

**Visual:** Zoom on the shelf — now filled with a few recipe cards/books, one pulled slightly forward and glowing (the one "in use"), the rest dim.

**Why it works:** Captures the real mechanic — skills aren't loaded upfront, they're loaded on demand — which is the single most-missed detail newbies have about how skills work.

## Production notes

- **One scene throughout.** Same kitchen illustration, same camera "zooms in" on the relevant element per slide, pulls back on slide 5. Consistency of the *setting* is what creates swipe pressure here — treat it as one continuous shot, not six unrelated cards.
- **Type size:** nothing under ~48pt at 1080 wide.
- **Slide 1 must work alone.** Test: does the cover stop a scroll with zero context that a carousel follows? If the empty shelf/hook doesn't read as "more is coming" at a glance, simplify the illustration further.
- **First hour:** reply to every comment — front-loaded engagement drives TikTok's initial push.
- **Series continuity:** open part 2 with the same kitchen image ("remember this?") to convert saves/follows from part 1 into part 2 views.
- **Metaphor honesty check:** the MCP slide's "plugged into somewhere specific" line is load-bearing — don't cut it for space. Without it, "appliance" alone underclaims what MCP actually does (it reaches external systems, not just performs a generic task).
